<p>While at the University of Washington, I developed a mooring based air-calibration system for oxygen optodes (<a href="https://bushinskyoceanlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Bushinsky-Emerson-2013-Marine-Chemistry.pdf">Bushinsky and Emerson, 2013</a>).  This system relied on pumping atmospheric air down to the sub-surface optode housing to calibrate the sensors once per day. </p>



<div class="wp-block-image is-style-default"><figure class="aligncenter size-full is-resized"><img src="https://bushinskyoceanlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/cropped-Picture1.png" alt="" class="wp-image-75" width="383" height="383"/><figcaption>The mooring based air-calibration system on the Cha'ba buoy</figcaption></figure></div>



<p>We subsequently modified this technique and developed Special-Oxygen-Sensor Argo floats equipped with oxygen sensors on long stalks that allowed calibration against atmospheric oxygen after every profile.&nbsp;</p>



<p>This not only provides high quality oxygen from Argo floats, fixing a major problem with sensor accuracy, but gave us the accuracy to discover and correct for an in-situ drift throughout a float’s 4-5 year deployment (<a href="https://bushinskyoceanlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Emerson-Bushinsky-2014-Oceanography.pdf">Emerson and Bushinsky, 2014</a>; <a href="https://bushinskyoceanlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Bushinsky-et-al.-2016-Limnology-and-Oceanography-Methods.pdf">Bushinsky et al., 2016</a>).</p>
