## Research

I work with autonomous ocean observations, especially Biogeochemical Argo floats, to improve data quality control and develop research-ready datasets for ocean biogeochemistry.

My current research contributes to BGC-Argo+, a quality-controlled global compilation of biogeochemical Argo float data with secondary quality control and derived parameters. I focus on identifying, quantifying, and correcting biases in biogeochemical Argo float observations, then developing those corrected measurements into a new dataset for broader scientific use.

I also build tools for working with large float datasets. One current project is an outlier-removal interface for Argo float data that lets users move through floats and profiles, manually flag suspect data, and export those decisions for downstream quality-control workflows.

I am especially interested in how improved float data products can support studies of open-ocean carbon, oxygen, pH, nitrate, and related biogeochemical variability. In future work, I plan to pair satellite and float observations to explore ocean greening, investigating whether observed changes in surface phytoplankton bloom phenology and magnitude reflect broader biogeochemical changes in the upper ocean and biological carbon export.

## Background

I am based in the Department of Oceanography at UH Mānoa, where I work with the HI-Cycles group on ocean observing, data quality, and open-ocean biogeochemistry. My day-to-day work is a mix of float data analysis and collaborative projects that use BGC-Argo observations at regional to global scales.

During my undergraduate studies, I worked with CMIP climate models to investigate differences in Pacific water-column stratification. I also used machine learning models trained with biogeochemical Argo float data to predict biogeochemical properties along Seaglider tracks in the Southern Ocean.

Alongside that research, I worked as a float technician in the University of Washington Argo Float Lab from 2020 to 2022. There, I helped assemble, calibrate, bench test, and quality-control core and biogeochemical floats before deployment. I also supported instrument inventory and shipping, entered and checked technical data for quality control, used Python to analyze float data and diagnose failure modes, and helped train new hires in float assembly, maintenance, and data workflows.

These experiences complement my work with autonomous observing systems by connecting float data products back to shipboard sampling, instrument deployment, and oceanographic field operations.

I am a Uehiro Center for the Advancement of Oceanography Fellow and have been involved in graduate student service through Nā Kama Kai.
